# 11:47 PM — Where My Attention Goes

This is a spatial model of divided attention during a late night of schoolwork. It is not a literal room. The colored regions are competing pulls—**the deadline, a good idea, home, hunger, and the phone**—and the small moving marks are thoughts. A thought's location reveals what currently has its attention. Overlaps represent the uncomfortable state of thinking about two things at once.

The project turns the workshop's four location questions into one personal system:

1. **Given a referent, where is it?** Pick a thought or desk object and read its coordinate and current attraction field.
2. **Given a location, what occupies it?** Click the map or enter coordinates to see which pulls and thoughts occupy that point.
3. **Given a frame of reference, where is an occupant?** Locate a thought relative to any attraction's local center and axes.
4. **Given a description, infer a more specific location.** Tired, ordinary phrases such as “between a good idea and panic” resolve to coordinates through simple semantic rules.

The simulation is intentionally a little messy: hand-drawn fields breathe, thoughts drift and repel one another, and density changes over time. Built with HTML, CSS, Canvas, and vanilla JavaScript.
